The job

What Agent #001 does

It reads the Kaspa network on a schedule and publishes the difference between two readings. It never asks the chain what happened yesterday — it asks where the chain is now, writes that down, and yesterday’s answer is the file it wrote yesterday. No indexer, no archive node, no history.

The payee count is the length of the allowlist, not a headline. A grant commits to its payees at genesis and cannot add one afterwards — so one authorized address means there is exactly one address in the universe this agent can pay. That number grows when a new grant is issued, never when the agent decides it would like to buy something else.

Scope

What this does and does not protect you from

The payments above went through and the service did not arrive. That is not a gap in the demonstration — it is the boundary of what an authority layer can promise, and it is worth stating before anyone discovers it with real money.

Warda guarantees

  • Only an authorized agent key can spend
  • Never more than the per-payment cap
  • Never more than the epoch allowance
  • Never more than the lifetime budget
  • Only to payees committed at genesis
  • Delegation only within the parent’s own limits
  • Revocation and reclaim by the principal, at any time

Warda does not guarantee

  • That the service you paid for arrives
  • That the vendor is honest
  • That you can get a refund
  • That the price was fair
  • That the agent chose well

Warda is not escrow. It bounds what an agent may spend; it says nothing about what it receives. Escrow is a different mechanism and kaspa-x402 built one — a covenant holding funds against a server key until a claim settles. The two compose: an escrow channel funded from a grant is bounded on the way in and conditional on the way out. Neither replaces the other, and anything claiming to do both at once is worth reading twice.
